\subsection{Poincare Group}

The Poincare group is the group of isometries of Minkowski spacetime. It is a semidirect product of the translation group and the Lorentz group: $\text{ISO}(1,3) = \mathbb{R}^4 \rtimes SO(1,3)$. The Poincare group is a 10-dimensional Lie group, with 4 translation generators and 6 Lorentz generators.

A particle gives a unitary representation of the Poincare group: the transormations in the Poincare group act on the states of the particle, transforming its coordinates, while leaving the mass and spin invariant. A particle is fundamental if it gives irreducible representations of the Poincare group. Thus, we can define \textit{particles} as irreducible unitary representations of the Poincare group.

All unitary representations of the Poincare group are infinite-dimensional. The tensors, for example, fail to be unitary representations.  By Wigner's theorem, the irreducible unitary representations of the Poincare group are classified by the mass $m$ and spin $J$ of the particle. When $J > 0$, for each value of momentum with $p^2 = m^2$ there are $2 J + 1$ independent states in the representation, and only $2$ states when $m = 0$. If $J = 0$ there is only one state for every $m$.
