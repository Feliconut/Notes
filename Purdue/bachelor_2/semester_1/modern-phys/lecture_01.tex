\section{Deficiencies of Classical Physics}
\subsection{EM wave from Maxwell's equations}
Maxwell's equations in free space
\begin{align*}
	\nabla \cdot \vec B &= 0\\
	\nabla \cdot \vec E &= 0\\
	\nabla \times \vec E + \frac{\partial \vec B }{\partial t }  &= 0\\
	\nabla \times \vec B - \mu \varepsilon \frac{\partial \vec E }{\partial t }  &= 0
.\end{align*}

Assume a harmonic time dependence $e^{i \omega t}$

Rewrite the equations:
\begin{align*}
	\nabla \times \vec E - i \omega B &= 0\\
	\nabla \times \vec{B} + i \omega \mu \varepsilon E &= 0
.\end{align*}

Take the curl of the first equation:
\begin{align*}
	\nabla \times \nabla \times \vec{E} -i \omega \nabla \times \vec{B}  &= 0 \\
	- \nabla^2 E - \omega^2 \mu\varepsilon E &= 0 \\
	\left( \nabla^2 + \omega^2 \mu\varepsilon  \right) \vec{E} &= 0
.\end{align*}

Now we solve the equation in 1D. 

The solution is of the form $E = Ae^{ikx}+Be^{-ikx}$, where $k = \omega\sqrt{\mu\varepsilon } $

\begin{align*}
	\frac{\partial E }{\partial x} &= i k A e ^{ikx} - ikBe^{-ikx}\\
	\frac{\partial ^2 E }{\partial x^2 } &= -k^{2} A e ^{ikx } - i k^2 B e^{-i h x }= - k^2 E \\
	\frac{\partial ^2 E }{\partial x^2 } + \omega^2 \mu\varepsilon E &= 0 \implies k^2 = \omega^2 \mu\varepsilon
.\end{align*}

We examine the solution $E = A \sin\left( kx \pm \omega t + \phi \right) $. We see:

\begin{equation}
E = A \sin\left( k\left( x \pm \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu\varepsilon } t }+ \phi \right)  \right) 
.\end{equation} 

When in vacuum, the wave speed is $c = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_0\varepsilon_0} }$. When not in vacuum, the speed is determined by $v = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu\varepsilon} } = \frac{c}{n}$ where $n$ is the index of refraction.

The valocity is free space is always $c$, which is a breakdown of \logseq{Galilean Relativity}.
