\note{2}{Wed 31 May 2023 18:37}{Convergence Theorems}

In Measure Theory, a sequence of functions $(f_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$ in some measure space $(X, \mathcal{F}, \mu)$ may converge to some $f$ as its limit. The notion of "convergence" is a very versatile one; the are many different formulations of convergence available. 

Those different convergence notions may be (partially) ranked with respect to their "strength", namely how "uniform" the convergence is, and how "large" is the set where convergence occurs.

The definitions are as follows:

\begin{itemize}
	\item [Uniform] We say $f_n \to f$ \textit{uniformly} if for all $\varepsilon>0$ there exists $N>0$ such that for all $x \in X$ and all $n > N $ we have $|f_n(x) - f(x)| < \varepsilon$.
	\item [PW] We say $f_n \to f$ \textit{pointwise} if for all  $x \in X$, $f_n(x) \to f(x)$ as $n \to \infty $.
	\item [AE] We say $f_n \to f$ \textit{pointwise almost everywhere} if there exists a set $E \subset X$ with $\mu(E) = 0$ such that for all $x \in X \setminus E$, $f_n(x) \to f(x)$ as $n \to \infty $.
	\item [AU] We say $f_n \to f$ \textit{almost uniformly} if for all $\varepsilon>0$ there exists a set  $E \subset X$ with $\mu(E) < \varepsilon$ such that $f_n \to f$ uniformly outside $E$.
	\item [$L^\infty $ ] We say $f_n \to f$ \textit{uniformly almost everywhere}, or \textit{in $L^\infty $ norm}, if there exists a set $E \subset X$ with $\mu(E) = 0$, such that $f_n \to f$ uniformly outside $E$.
	\item [$L^p$ ] We say $f_n \to f$ \textit{in $L^p$ norm} if $\int_X |f_n - f|^p \to 0$ as $n \to  \infty $.
	\item [M] We say $f_n \to f$ \textit{in measure} if for any $\varepsilon>0$ and $E_{n, \varepsilon}:= \{x \in X: |f_n(x) - f(x)| > \varepsilon\} $, we have $\mu(E_{n, \varepsilon} \to 0$ as $n \to \infty $.
	\item [dist] We say $f_n \to f$ \textit{in distribution}, if for all $a \in \mathbb{R}$ where $f $ is continuous at $a$, we have $\mu\left( \{x \in X: f_n(x) < a\}  \right) \to  \mu\left( \{x \in X: f(x) < a\}  \right) $ as $n \to \infty $.
\end{itemize}
